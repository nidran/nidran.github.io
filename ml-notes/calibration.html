Evaluation & Metrics · quick answer · 1 min read

Calibration

Say it in 30s: A model is calibrated if predicted probabilities match reality — among cases scored 0.7, the event happens about 70% of the time. AUC and ranking metrics only care about order; calibration cares whether the number means what it says. Fix miscalibration on held-out data with Platt scaling or isotonic regression; always check a reliability diagram before shipping scores into pricing or risk decisions.

Key points

  • Reliability diagram: bin predictions, plot mean predicted vs observed frequency; perfect calibration hugs the diagonal.
  • Expected Calibration Error (ECE): weighted average gap between predicted and observed rates across bins — a single scalar summary.
  • Common pattern: boosted trees and deep nets often overconfident; naive Bayes pushes to 0/1; logistic regression is usually reasonable out of the box when trained on log-loss.

Brier score (proper scoring rule): 1n∑i(p^i−yi)2\frac{1}{n}\sum_i (\hat p_i - y_i)^2n1​∑i​(p^​i​−yi​)2 — penalizes both calibration and sharpness.

Post-hoc fixes (fit on validation): Platt scaling p^=σ(as+b)\hat p = \sigma(as + b)p^​=σ(as+b) on scores sss; temperature scaling softmax(z/T)\mathrm{softmax}(z/T)softmax(z/T) for KKK-class logits zzz. Proper rules (log-loss, Brier) reward honest probabilities on hold-out data.

Common follow-ups / traps

  • "Great AUC — are we done?" → Not if you threshold on the probability or multiply it into EV math; check calibration.
  • "Can I calibrate on the test set?" → No — fit the calibrator on validation only, then report on test once.
