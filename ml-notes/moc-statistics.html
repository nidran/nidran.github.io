Statistics

Inference, hypothesis tests, and experiment design: how to tell whether a result is real and how to measure causal impact.

#Chapters

  • Probability & Statistics — probability rules, Bayes and base rates, key distributions, LLN/CLT, estimators, MLE vs MAP, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, multiple testing, bootstrap
  • A-B Testing & Experimentation — design, sample size and power, SRM, peeking, novelty effects, interference, CUPED, ratio metrics, heterogeneous effects, holdouts, quasi-experiments, offline vs online metrics, interleaving

  • Fundamentals — bias-variance, true vs empirical risk (Foundations)

#If asked...

  • ...about a rare-event classifier or a "99% accurate test": write down prior, sensitivity, and false positive rate, apply Bayes, and answer in natural frequencies; precision depends on prevalence.
  • ...what a p-value or confidence interval means: p-value is the probability of data this extreme given H0H_0H0​; a CI is a procedure with long-run coverage. Neither is the probability that a hypothesis or parameter value is true.
  • ...which statistical test to use: name the outcome type, the design (paired or independent), and the sample size; then the assumption most likely to fail, usually independence.
  • ...how much data an experiment needs: baseline, minimum detectable effect, α\alphaα, power; n≈16σ2/δ2n \approx 16\sigma^2/\delta^2n≈16σ2/δ2 per arm at 80% power and 5% significance; whole weeks.
  • ...whether to ship a result that looks significant: check SRM, peeking, multiple metrics, novelty effects, and guardrails before the effect size.
  • ...an experiment where users affect each other: say that no-interference fails; propose cluster, geo, or switchback designs and state which estimand is measured.
  • ...a change that cannot be randomized: difference-in-differences or synthetic control, with the key assumption and a placebo check.
