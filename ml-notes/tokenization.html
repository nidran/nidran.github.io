Tokenization: BPE, WordPiece & SentencePiece

Say it in 30s: LLMs do not read characters or words natively — a tokenizer maps text to a fixed vocabulary of subword IDs. BPE (GPT) and SentencePiece (T5, Llama) grow vocab by merging the most frequent pairs; WordPiece (BERT) picks merges that raise training likelihood. Rare words become several tokens, common fragments become one. Token count drives cost, context limits, and eval quirks.

Key points

  • BPE: start from bytes/chars, iteratively merge the most frequent pair; used in GPT-2/3/4 lineage.
  • WordPiece: merge pairs that maximize likelihood of training data (greedy); ## prefix marks continuations in BERT.
  • SentencePiece: treats the sentence as a raw byte stream with a special space marker (▁); no language-specific pre-tokenization — same code for Chinese and English.
  • OOV handling: no unknown-token dead ends; anything is a composition of subwords (may be long for rare strings).
  • Pitfalls: leading spaces, punctuation, and casing change tokenization; "same word" in prompts can split differently after fine-tuning.

The math

BPE: repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent symbol pair until vocab size VVV. Example corpus aa, aa, ab: counts a|a×2, a|b×1 → merge aa; repeat on aa, aa, ab until VVV is met. WordPiece: merge pair ababab with score count(ab)/(count(a)⋅count(b))\mathrm{count}(ab)/(\mathrm{count}(a)\cdot\mathrm{count}(b))count(ab)/(count(a)⋅count(b)) (pointwise mutual-information style). Unigram LM: choose a vocab to maximize ∑xlog⁡∑segmentations s∏t∈sp(t)\sum_{x}\log\sum_{\text{segmentations }s}\prod_{t\in s}p(t)∑x​log∑segmentations s​∏t∈s​p(t) over all segmentations of training strings. Trade-off: larger VVV → shorter sequences (fewer tokens per doc) but more embedding rows V⋅dV\cdot dV⋅d and a larger softmax.

Common follow-ups / traps

  • "Why subwords not words?" → finite vocab, open vocabulary, morphology shared across words; trade-off is longer sequences for rare terms.
  • "Does tokenizer affect LLM eval?" → yes — BLEU/F1 on tokens vs detokenized strings; different models are not comparable token-for-token.
  • "Special tokens?" → BOS, EOS, PAD, mask, chat template markers; they occupy vocab slots and affect attention masks.
