Generative vs Discriminative

Say it in 30s: Discriminative models learn the boundary directly — P(y | x) — they only answer "which class." Generative models learn each class's shape — P(x | y) and P(y) — then use Bayes' rule to derive P(y | x), so they can also generate new x.

The canonical pair

  • Logistic Regression (discriminative) vs Naive Bayes (generative).
  • Discriminative usually wins with ample data (optimizes the thing you care about).
  • Generative wins with little data or when you need to sample, detect outliers, or handle missing features.

Examples

  • Discriminative: logistic regression, SVMs, most neural net classifiers, random forests.
  • Generative: Naive Bayes, GMMs, HMMs, and modern generative models (VAEs, diffusion, LLMs).

The math

Bayes rule:

p(y∣x)=p(x∣y) p(y)p(x)∝p(x∣y) p(y).p(y\mid x)=\frac{p(x\mid y)\,p(y)}{p(x)} \propto p(x\mid y)\,p(y).p(y∣x)=p(x)p(x∣y)p(y)​∝p(x∣y)p(y).

Discriminative models estimate p(y∣x)p(y\mid x)p(y∣x) (or a score/decision boundary) directly — no explicit p(x∣y)p(x\mid y)p(x∣y).

Generative / joint:

p(x,y)=p(x∣y) p(y),y^=arg⁡max⁡y p(y∣x)=arg⁡max⁡y p(x∣y) p(y).p(x,y)=p(x\mid y)\,p(y),\qquad \hat{y}=\arg\max_y\, p(y\mid x)=\arg\max_y\, p(x\mid y)\,p(y).p(x,y)=p(x∣y)p(y),y^​=argymax​p(y∣x)=argymax​p(x∣y)p(y).

Shared-covariance Gaussians (LDA): p(x∣y=k)=N(μk,Σ)p(x\mid y=k)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\Sigma)p(x∣y=k)=N(μk​,Σ) with common Σ\SigmaΣ. Log-posterior ratio for classes k,ℓk,\ellk,ℓ:

ln⁡p(y=k∣x)p(y=ℓ∣x)=w⊤x+b\ln\frac{p(y=k\mid x)}{p(y=\ell\mid x)} = w^\top x + blnp(y=ℓ∣x)p(y=k∣x)​=w⊤x+b

because the quadratic x⊤Σ−1xx^\top\Sigma^{-1}xx⊤Σ−1x cancels in the ratio — log-odds linear in xxx, same functional form as logistic regression.

Trap: "generative" in the classic sense (models P(x,y)) is a superset idea that also covers today's generative AI — be ready to bridge both meanings.
