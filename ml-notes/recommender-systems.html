Recommender Systems

Recommendations are the canonical ranking problem: you only see feedback on what you showed, the catalog is enormous, and the metric you optimize is rarely the metric the business cares about.

Every large consumer product eventually becomes a recommender. Search, feeds, ads, marketplaces, and "similar items" are the same skeleton with different labels: retrieve a tractable candidate set from a sea of items, rank them with rich cross-features, then re-rank for policy, diversity, and freshness. This chapter is the technical depth behind that funnel — explicit vs implicit feedback, matrix factorization and implicit ALS, two-tower retrieval with corrected sampled softmax, ranking model families, bias correction, cold start, and how offline ranking metrics relate to online engagement. Use ML System Design Framework for the design spine.

What you'll be able to say after this chapter
  • Frame recsys as retrieval + ranking with different latency budgets and label semantics (explicit vs implicit).
  • Write down matrix factorization with L2 regularization and the Hu–Koren–Volinsky objective for implicit feedback.
  • Explain the multistage funnel (billions → thousands → tens) and where two-tower ANN vs heavy rankers sit.
  • Name in-batch negatives, sampled softmax, and log-frequency (logQ) correction for unbiased retrieval training.
  • Diagnose exposure, position, and popularity bias — and cite IPW, exploration, and re-ranking as fixes.
  • Connect recall@k, NDCG, and coverage offline to watch time / retention online without conflating them.

#Problem framing: what are we optimizing?

A recommender answers: given this user and context, which items should we show, in what order? That question hides three separate decisions interviewers expect you to unpack.

Feedback type. Explicit feedback is a deliberate rating or vote (stars, thumbs). Labels are sparse but semantically clear. Implicit feedback is behavior: clicks, plays, dwell time, purchases, skips. It is abundant but ambiguous — a skip is not a hate, and silence is not disinterest if the item was never shown. Most production systems are implicit-first because coverage of the catalog matters more than calibrated star predictions.

Relevance. "Relevant" is not universal. For search, relevance is query–document match. For a For You feed, it is often predicted engagement (watch time, completion rate) plus creator health guardrails. For e-commerce, it may be purchase probability × margin. Say out loud which objective you're proxying and what you sacrifice.

Business objectives vs ML proxies. Optimizing click-through rate can inflate clickbait; optimizing watch time can favor long passive videos; optimizing GMV can ignore satisfaction. Strong answers name a north-star (e.g. D7 retention) and treat model heads as proxies tuned via A/B Testing & Experimentation. Multi-task ranking (CTR + dwell + share) is the usual compromise — see Models & Loss Functions Reference for MMoE and listwise losses, not duplicated here.

Signal Pros Cons
Explicit ratings Clear polarity Sparse, selection-biased reviewers
Implicit clicks Dense Exposure-biased; position-biased
Dwell / watch time Closer to satisfaction Noisy; background playback
Purchases / GMV Revenue-aligned Extremely sparse; delayed
Memory hook

Implicit feedback answers "what did they do when they saw it?" — not "what would they do if we showed everything."

Interview angle

Weak: "We predict whether they'll like it." Strong: we predict expected watch time (or click) conditional on exposure, train on logged impressions with position debiasing, and validate online on session length with diversity guardrails.

Rapid fire — Framing

Explicit = sparse, clear; implicit = dense, ambiguous. Relevance is product-specific (match vs engagement vs revenue). Offline ranking metrics proxy online north-stars — never confuse them. Multi-task heads separate CTR from depth-of-engagement. Name exposure before you talk about negatives.

#Content-based vs collaborative filtering

Content-based filtering recommends items similar to what the user liked, using item features (tags, text embeddings, visual embeddings from a CNN/ViT). It works on day one for new items with metadata and does not need a crowd — but it struggles with taste nuance and serendipity ("users who liked A also liked B" when A and B look unrelated).

Collaborative filtering (CF) exploits interaction patterns: users who agreed in the past will agree again. User–user CF is rare at scale (sparse neighborhoods). Item–item CF ("customers who bought X also bought Y") still appears in baskets. Matrix factorization and embedding retrieval are the scalable CF story: latent factors compress co-occurrence structure.

Hybrid production systems almost always blend both: content embeddings for cold-start items, CF/two-tower for personalization, plus popularity/trending priors. Link: Embeddings & Vector Search for ANN serving details.

Neighborhood methods still appear in interviews: item–item cosine on co-purchase vectors is O(catalog)O(\text{catalog})O(catalog) to precompute but cheap at serve for "similar products." At billion scale you precompute top-MMM neighbors offline and store in a key-value store.

Interview angle

When asked "content vs CF," answer with a timeline: content dominates minute zero, CF dominates month three, hybrid forever.

#Matrix factorization

Model user uuu and item iii with vectors pu,qi∈Rd\mathbf{p}_u, \mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^dpu​,qi​∈Rd. For explicit ratings ruir_{ui}rui​, a standard objective is regularized squared error on observed entries O\mathcal{O}O:

min⁡P,Q∑(u,i)∈O(rui−pu⊤qi)2+λ∑u∥pu∥2+λ∑i∥qi∥2\min_{\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Q}} \sum_{(u,i)\in\mathcal{O}} \left( r_{ui} - \mathbf{p}_u^\top \mathbf{q}_i \right)^2 + \lambda \sum_u \lVert \mathbf{p}_u \rVert^2 + \lambda \sum_i \lVert \mathbf{q}_i \rVert^2P,Qmin​(u,i)∈O∑​(rui​−pu⊤​qi​)2+λu∑​∥pu​∥2+λi∑​∥qi​∥2

This is low-rank factorization of the partially observed rating matrix. Alternating Least Squares (ALS) fixes one side and solves linear least squares for the other — embarrassingly parallel, great on explicit data in Spark. SGD samples (u,i)(u,i)(u,i) pairs and updates with a learning rate; flexible for extensions (side features, neural layers).

#Implicit feedback: confidence weights (Hu / Koren / Volinsky)

For implicit data, let xuix_{ui}xui​ be raw interaction strength (e.g. play count), pui=1[xui>0]p_{ui} = \mathbb{1}[x_{ui} > 0]pui​=1[xui​>0] the preference indicator, and confidence weights cuic_{ui}cui​:

cui=1+α xuic_{ui} = 1 + \alpha\, x_{ui}cui​=1+αxui​

where α\alphaα scales how much you trust repeated consumption. The weighted ALS objective minimizes:

min⁡P,Q∑u,icui(pui−pu⊤qi)2+λ∑u∥pu∥2+λ∑i∥qi∥2\min_{\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Q}} \sum_{u,i} c_{ui}\left( p_{ui} - \mathbf{p}_u^\top \mathbf{q}_i \right)^2 + \lambda \sum_u \lVert \mathbf{p}_u \rVert^2 + \lambda \sum_i \lVert \mathbf{q}_i \rVert^2P,Qmin​u,i∑​cui​(pui​−pu⊤​qi​)2+λu∑​∥pu​∥2+λi∑​∥qi​∥2

Intuition: observed clicks are strong positives; missing entries are negatives with low confidence — not "definitely disliked." Modern stacks often replace pure MF with two-tower neural CF, but interviewers still expect you to write this objective and explain ALS vs SGD.

Common mistake

Treating every unclicked impression as a hard negative in implicit MF — that assumes uniform exposure and destroys tail-item recall.

Memory hook

Implicit MF: positives are confident; unobserved is "maybe negative," weighted by cuic_{ui}cui​, not 0/1 dogma.

Rapid fire — Matrix factorization

Dot product of user/item embeddings = score. Explicit MF: squared error + L2 on O\mathcal{O}O. Implicit: pui∈{0,1}p_{ui}\in\{0,1\}pui​∈{0,1} with confidence cui=1+αxuic_{ui}=1+\alpha x_{ui}cui​=1+αxui​. ALS alternates closed-form blocks; SGD samples pairs. Neural MF adds features and nonlinearities → two-tower retrieval.

#The multistage funnel

You cannot score a billion videos with a 500-feature DCN at 50ms. Production recsys is a funnel:

flowchart LR
  C["Catalog\n~10⁹ items"] --> R["Retrieval\n~10³–10⁴ candidates"]
  R --> K["Ranking\n~10²–10³ scored"]
  K --> RR["Re-ranking\n~10–50 shown"]

Typical latency budgets (order-of-magnitude): retrieval 10–30ms (mostly ANN + light models), ranking 30–80ms (heavy GPU/CPU ensemble), re-rank & blend <10ms (rules + MMR). Recall dominates retrieval (miss the right item and ranking never sees it); precision and calibration dominate ranking; diversity, freshness, policy dominate re-rank.

Stage Catalog → out Primary metric Model class
Candidate generation 10810^8108–10910^9109 → 10310^3103–10410^4104 Recall@k CF, two-tower + ANN, graph walk, BM25
Ranking thousands → hundreds NDCG / AUC GBDT, Wide&Deep, DCN, DIN, MMoE
Re-ranking hundreds → ~10–50 Diversity, policy MMR, rules, lightweight scorer

Union multiple retrieval sources: personalized ANN, trending, subscriptions, exploration buckets, editorial. The ranker learns to merge evidence; retrieval diversity improves recall.

Quantitative intuition. If each retrieval channel contributes 500 candidates and you dedupe, you might feed 1500–2500 unique IDs to the ranker. Truncate to top 200 by a cheap pre-score (dot product or heuristic) if the ranker budget is tight. Latency is often dominated by feature lookup and cross feature materialization, not the final MLP forward pass — cache hot user histories and precomputed item stats.

For search-plus-feed products, query understanding (intent, language) feeds the user tower as context features; the same two-tower index serves browse and search with different tower inputs.

Interview angle

Draw the funnel before naming a model. Say approximate candidate counts and where ANN runs vs full cross-features.

Rapid fire — Funnel

Retrieve wide, rank deep, re-rank for product rules. ANN at billions; cross-features only on thousands. Multiple recall channels merged before rank. Latency budget forces decoupled item embeddings offline.

#Two-tower retrieval

Architecture. A user tower fu(user features)→u∈Rdf_u(\text{user features}) \to \mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^dfu​(user features)→u∈Rd and item tower fi(item features)→v∈Rdf_i(\text{item features}) \to \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^dfi​(item features)→v∈Rd. Score s(u,i)=u⊤vs(u,i) = \mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}s(u,i)=u⊤v (sometimes cosine after L2 norm). Serving win: precompute all item vectors offline, build an ANN index (HNSW, ScaNN, FAISS); online, compute u\mathbf{u}u once, one ANN query → top-kkk. No per-pair forward pass.

Training with in-batch negatives. For a batch of BBB (user, positive item) pairs, negatives are other items in the batch. Loss is often sampled softmax / InfoNCE:

L=−∑(u,i+)ln⁡exp⁡(u⊤v+/τ)exp⁡(u⊤v+/τ)+∑j∈Nexp⁡(u⊤vj/τ)\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{(u,i^+)} \ln \frac{\exp(\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}^+ / \tau)}{\exp(\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}^+ / \tau) + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}} \exp(\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}_j / \tau)}L=−(u,i+)∑​lnexp(u⊤v+/τ)+∑j∈N​exp(u⊤vj​/τ)exp(u⊤v+/τ)​

logQ correction. Popular items appear as negatives more often in in-batch sampling, biasing the partition function. Importance sampling correction subtracts log item frequency:

ln⁡exp⁡(u⊤vj)∑kexp⁡(u⊤vk−ln⁡Q(vk))\ln \frac{\exp(\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}_j)}{\sum_k \exp(\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}_k - \ln Q(v_k))}ln∑k​exp(u⊤vk​−lnQ(vk​))exp(u⊤vj​)​

where Q(vk)∝Q(v_k) \proptoQ(vk​)∝ item exposure frequency (estimated from logs). Without correction, retrieval over-promotes head items — a classic popularity bias amplifier.

Hard negative mining (same category, rejected impressions) and curriculum learning appear in mature systems; start with in-batch + logQ in interviews.

Memory hook

Two-tower = dot product retrieval; items offline in ANN, users online once — decoupling is the scale trick.

See also the card Two-Tower Retrieval.

Rapid fire — Two-tower

User tower once per request; item tower offline → ANN. In-batch negatives scale training. Sampled softmax with temperature τ\tauτ. logQ debiases frequent negatives. BPR/triplet losses are pairwise alternatives — see Loss Functions.

#Ranking models (brief catalog)

After retrieval, you have budget for user×item crosses: match scores, co-occurrence, real-time context, sequence encodings.

Family Idea When it shines
GBDT ([[gradient-boosting Gradient Boosting]]) Binned dense + sparse features
Wide & Deep Linear crosses + DNN Memorize frequent rules + generalize
DCN / DeepFM Explicit feature crossing High-cardinality categoricals
DIN / DIEN Attention over user history Session-heavy feeds
MMoE Shared trunk, gating per task CTR + watch time + share jointly

Train on logged impressions with sampled negatives for classification, or listwise / pairwise objectives for pure ranking — details in Models & Loss Functions Reference and Ranking Metrics. Calibration matters if scores feed auctions or multi-stage fusion: see Calibration.

#Pointwise, pairwise, and listwise training

Pointwise: each (user, item) row is a binary or regression label — click, watch seconds. Fast to engineer; ignores slate context. Pairwise: prefer i+i^+i+ over i−i^-i− (BPR, hinge) — aligns with implicit feedback but ignores position among many positives. Listwise: optimize entire impression list (LambdaRank, softmax over slate, ApproxNDCG) — closest to NDCG but heavier and sensitive to exposure bias in logs. Production often trains pointwise multi-task heads for scale, then listwise fine-tuning on a smaller reranked set.

Feature families at rank time (memorize the four buckets from ML System Design Framework):

Family Examples Role
User age cohort, activity rate, embeddings of history Prior taste
Item category, creator tier, content embedding Prior quality
Cross match scores, co-watch counts, same-creator Personalization
Context time of day, device, geo, position (train only) Situational

Real-time crosses (last 5 clicks → attention over candidate categories) are why DIN-style sequence encoders beat static profiles on feeds.

Go deeper (optional)

Graph-based retrieval (PinSage, item graphs) propagates signal along co-engagement edges — strong when CF density is high but cold nodes need content side information. See Graph Neural Networks for message-passing intuition.

Interview angle

Don't recite every architecture. Pick GBDT baseline → justify deep ranker for sequence/cross features → mention multi-task if the product cares about more than clicks.

#Negative sampling, position, and exposure bias

Negative sampling for implicit CTR: sample items the user did not interact with, often stratified (uniform + hard negatives). Too-easy negatives inflate AUC but hurt retrieval recall; too-hard without debiasing confuses exposure with dislike.

Position bias: higher slots get more clicks regardless of quality. Fixes: (1) train with position as a feature, zero it at serving; (2) inverse propensity weighting (IPW) — weight each click by 1/p^(click∣pos)1 / \hat{p}(\text{click} \mid \text{pos})1/p^​(click∣pos) from a position model; (3) randomized exploration slots to estimate propensities.

Exposure / selection bias: you only observe labels for recommended items — a feedback loop. Unbiased learning-to-rank and exploration (ε-greedy buckets, Thompson sampling on arms) prevent the model from only reinforcing past winners. Connect to Data Leakage: future engagement used as a label for past decisions must respect causality.

Rapid fire — Bias

Skip ≠ hate; missing ≠ negative without exposure model. Position feature trick at serve-time zero. IPW reweights clicks by slot propensity. Exploration breaks the filter bubble in training data.

#Cold start, diversity, freshness, exploration

User cold start: no history → onboarding tastes, demographics, popular/trending, or content-based from first clicks. Item cold start: metadata / content embedding at upload → immediately in ANN; CF signal accumulates with impressions. Card: Cold Start.

Diversity: Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) and determinantal point processes (DPP) re-rank to reduce redundancy. Freshness: inject time-decayed or "new creator" slots in re-rank. Exploration: multi-armed bandits on item clusters or new uploads; balance regret vs learning — often 1–5% traffic.

Memory hook

Cold item = content vector day zero; cold user = prior + trending until history exists.

#Feedback loops and popularity bias

Positive feedback reinforces items already shown → rich get richer. Mitigations: debiased training (IPW, causal estimators), propensity-scored negatives, exploration traffic, and coverage metrics in offline eval. Monitor creator concentration (Gini on impressions) as a guardrail alongside engagement.

#Exploration as a first-class system component

Reserve 1–5% of impressions for uniform random, Thompson sampling on cluster arms, or new-item buckets. Exploration traffic is not wasted — it generates unbiased counterfactual data for propensity models and prevents retrieval collapse to yesterday's winners. Trade-off: short-term CTR dip vs long-tail recall. Mention regret qualitatively; you do not need full bandit proofs in system design rounds.

Engineering requirements: log explore_flag, route explore slates through the same ranker with exploration-only features zeroed, and power A/B tests on explore rate separately from model changes.

#Offline vs online metrics

Offline What it measures Gap
Recall@k Did the right item appear in top-kkk? Ignores position within slate
NDCG@k Graded relevance with position discount Needs labels; still not causality
Coverage Fraction of catalog recommended Anti-popularity-bias guardrail
MAP / MRR Binary relevance ranking Search-style

Online: session length, retention, revenue — via controlled experiments. Offline lifts in NDCG do not guarantee online wins if exploration policy or slate composition changed. Always pre-register guardrails (reports, diversity).

Rapid fire — Metrics

Recall@k for retrieval quality; NDCG for ranker; coverage for tail health. Offline optimizes logged policy; online tests new policy with A/B. Connect proxy to north-star explicitly.

#Worked design sketch: short-video For You feed

Scope. 200M DAU, 10910^9109 short videos, ~100ms end-to-end, mostly implicit feedback.

  1. Objective. Multi-task ranker: P(click)P(\text{click})P(click), E[watch time]E[\text{watch time}]E[watch time], P(share)P(\text{share})P(share); online north-star = DAU-weighted watch time + retention guardrail.
  2. Data. Impression logs with position, dwell, user/item IDs; content embeddings at upload; point-in-time user history from ML in Production feature store patterns.
  3. Retrieval (~20ms). Two-tower (d=128d=128d=128) + HNSW on item vectors; union trending + following + explore bucket; k≈2000k \approx 2000k≈2000.
  4. Ranking (~70ms). MMoE over DCN crosses (user stats × video stats × match); train with position feature zeroed at serve; IPW on clicks.
  5. Re-rank (~10ms). MMR on creator/topic; freshness boost; policy filters (age, region).
  6. Eval. Offline: recall@1000 on held-out watches, NDCG@10 on impressions; online: A/B on watch time / D7 retention; monitor Data Drift vs Concept Drift weekly.

Capacity planning (back-of-envelope). 200M DAU × ~50 requests/day ≈ 101010^{10}1010 rank calls/day. If retrieval returns 2000 and rank scores 200, heavy rank forward passes are ≪\ll≪ full catalog — that is why the funnel exists. ANN index rebuild nightly on fresh item embeddings; incremental updates for new uploads every few minutes.

Failure modes to volunteer: retrieval recall collapse after embedding drift; ranker overfitting to position; explore budget cut amplifying popularity bias; creator starvation without MMR. Pair with production practices in ML in Production.

Interview angle

End with monitoring + retrain cadence unprompted — that's the staff signal ML System Design Framework emphasizes.

Rapid fire — Design sketch

10⁹ → 2k retrieve → rank 200 → show 20. Two-tower + unions. MMoE ranker with debiased clicks. MMR + freshness re-rank. NDCG offline, retention online, drift post-launch.
