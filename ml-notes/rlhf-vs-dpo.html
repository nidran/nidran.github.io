RLHF vs DPO

Say it in 30s: Both turn human (or AI) preferences into a better chat model, but RLHF does it in two explicit stages—train a reward model on pairwise comparisons, then optimize the policy with PPO while penalizing KL drift from a frozen reference—whereas DPO folds the reward into a classification loss on preferences and skips the reward model and RL loop entirely. RLHF is more flexible (multi-objective rewards, verifiers); DPO is simpler, cheaper, and usually more stable to train.

Key points

  • RLHF reward model: Bradley–Terry loss L=−log⁡σ(r(x,yw)−r(x,yl))\mathcal{L} = -\log \sigma(r(x,y_w) - r(x,y_l))L=−logσ(r(x,yw​)−r(x,yl​)) on chosen ywy_wyw​ vs rejected yly_lyl​.
  • RLHF policy: PPO maximizes expected reward minus β⋅KL(πθ ∥ πref)\beta \cdot \mathrm{KL}(\pi_\theta \,\Vert\, \pi_{\text{ref}})β⋅KL(πθ​∥πref​) to curb reward hacking and collapse.
  • DPO: directly increases πθ(yw∣x)/πref(yw∣x)\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)πθ​(yw​∣x)/πref​(yw​∣x) relative to yly_lyl​ via a sigmoid on log-likelihood ratios; β\betaβ plays the same role as the KL coefficient.
  • Variants: IPO (regression on margins), KTO (unpaired labels), ORPO (SFT + preference in one stage).

Common follow-ups / traps

  • "Why KL to the reference?" → without it the policy exploits the reward model (verbosity, sycophancy, gibberish that scores high).
  • "When prefer RLHF?" → need online feedback, tool-verified rewards, or a reward you can't write as a closed-form preference loss.
  • "DPO failure modes?" → distribution mismatch if SFT and preference data disagree; β\betaβ too small → hack, too large → no movement.
