BatchNorm vs LayerNorm

Say it in 30s: BatchNorm normalizes each feature across the batch (per channel in CNNs), then applies learned scale/shift γ,β\gamma,\betaγ,β. It speeds training and acts like mild regularization, but batch statistics make train and eval differ—you must switch modes. LayerNorm normalizes across features within each example, so it works for batch size 1 and sequence models. Transformers mostly use LayerNorm or RMSNorm; GroupNorm is the CV fix when batches are tiny.

Key points

  • BatchNorm (train): x^=(x−μB)/σB2+ϵ\hat{x} = (x - \mu_B)/\sqrt{\sigma_B^2+\epsilon}x^=(x−μB​)/σB2​+ϵ​, y=γx^+βy = \gamma\hat{x}+\betay=γx^+β, with μB,σB\mu_B,\sigma_BμB​,σB​ from the current mini-batch.
  • BatchNorm (eval): use running EMA μ,σ\mu,\sigmaμ,σ accumulated during training—not the current batch. model.eval() enables this.
  • LayerNorm: normalize over the hidden dimension for each token/sample independently—no cross-example dependence.
  • RMSNorm: like LayerNorm without mean centering: divide by RMS only; common in LLMs for speed.
  • Small batches: BatchNorm stats are noisy; prefer GroupNorm (normalize channel groups per spatial location) or LayerNorm.

The math

BatchNorm (per channel ccc): x^=(x−μB)/σB2+ϵ\hat{x}=(x-\mu_B)/\sqrt{\sigma_B^2+\epsilon}x^=(x−μB​)/σB2​+ϵ​, y=γx^+βy=\gamma\hat{x}+\betay=γx^+β; μB,σB\mu_B,\sigma_BμB​,σB​ are mean/variance over batch axis NNN (and spatial H×WH\times WH×W for N×C×H×WN\times C\times H\times WN×C×H×W). Eval: replace (μB,σB)(\mu_B,\sigma_B)(μB​,σB​) with EMA running stats. LayerNorm on N×T×DN\times T\times DN×T×D: reduce over hidden dim DDD (per token). RMSNorm: y=g⋅x/mean(x2)+ϵy = g \cdot x / \sqrt{\mathrm{mean}(x^2)+\epsilon}y=g⋅x/mean(x2)+ϵ​ (no mean centering). GroupNorm: split the CCC channels into GGG groups and, per sample (never across the batch), normalize over the group's channels plus H×WH\times WH×W. Axes cheat sheet (CNN N×C×H×WN\times C\times H\times WN×C×H×W): BN reduces over (N,H,W)(N,H,W)(N,H,W); GN over (C/G,H,W)(C/G, H, W)(C/G,H,W) inside each sample; transformers (N,T,D)(N,T,D)(N,T,D): LN/RMSNorm reduce over DDD.

Common follow-ups / traps

  • "Why does my BN model degrade at inference?" → Forgot eval(), or train/eval mismatch; running stats not updated if you never called train() during fit.
  • "Pre-LN vs Post-LN?" → Pre-LN (norm before sublayer) stabilizes deep transformers; Post-LN is original but harder to train without warmup.
