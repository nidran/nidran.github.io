LLM Training: Pretraining, Fine-Tuning & Alignment

From next-token prediction to models people actually want to talk to.

Modern chat LLMs are not one training run—they are a pipeline: self-supervised pretraining on web-scale text, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on instructions and demonstrations, then alignment that reshapes behavior toward helpful, honest, harmless responses. This chapter walks the objectives, the scaling math interviewers quote, and the alignment stack (RLHF, DPO, and cousins). It also covers when to full fine-tune vs LoRA vs prompt vs RAG, plus the distributed-training and memory accounting you need to reason about billion-parameter runs.

What you'll be able to say after this chapter
  • State the pretraining objective, link perplexity to average cross-entropy, and name what the data pipeline must fix (dedup, filtering, mixture).
  • Contrast Kaplan vs Chinchilla scaling and quote compute ≈6ND\approx 6ND≈6ND FLOPs and the ~20 tokens/param recipe.
  • Order the post-pretrain stages: SFT → preference optimization (RLHF or DPO) and say what breaks at each step.
  • Write the Bradley–Terry reward-model loss, the PPO + KL picture, and the DPO loss—and explain why DPO removes the explicit reward model.
  • Compute LoRA parameter savings for a layer and choose among full FT, LoRA, prompting, and RAG.
  • Estimate VRAM for training as ~16 bytes/param (mixed precision + Adam) and name data / tensor / pipeline parallelism and ZeRO/FSDP.

#Pretraining: next-token prediction at scale

Intuition. Pretraining teaches a transformer to compress language: given prior tokens, predict the next one. No human labels—every token is a label. That is self-supervised learning in the sense of Fundamentals: the supervision signal is manufactured from the text itself.

The objective is standard causal language modeling. For tokens x1,…,xTx_1,\ldots,x_Tx1​,…,xT​,

LLM=−1T∑t=1Tlog⁡pθ(xt∣x<t)\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}} = -\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p_\theta(x_t \mid x_{<t})LLM​=−T1​t=1∑T​logpθ​(xt​∣x<t​)

This is token-level cross-entropy against a one-hot target—the same MLE story as maximum-likelihood-estimation, summed over the vocabulary. Perplexity is the human-readable cousin:

PPL=exp⁡(LLM)\text{PPL} = \exp(\mathcal{L}_{\text{LM}})PPL=exp(LLM​)

If average cross-entropy is 2.0 nats, PPL ≈7.4\approx 7.4≈7.4: the model is as "surprised" as if it were choosing uniformly among ∼7\sim 7∼7 equally likely tokens at each step. Lower is better; PPL only compares models that share the same tokenizer and eval corpus.

Memory hook

Perplexity = exp(cross-entropy). Say "bits per token" in interviews only if you converted to log⁡2\log_2log2​.

Interview angle

Weak: "GPT predicts the next word." Strong: causal LM is joint density factorization ∏tp(xt∣x<t)\prod_t p(x_t\mid x_{<t})∏t​p(xt​∣x<t​) trained by token CE; perplexity is the held-out average surprise.

Architecture details live in Transformers & Attention and Self-Attention; here we care about data, compute, and what capability actually emerges.

#Data pipeline: dedup, filter, mix

Raw web text is poisonous without surgery. Production pipelines typically:

  1. Crawl & normalize — HTML stripping, language ID, boilerplate removal; Tokenization defines the unit of supervision.
  2. Deduplication — exact (hash) and fuzzy (MinHash/SimHash) dedup removes copy-paste and mirrors that otherwise dominate gradients.
  3. Quality filtering — heuristics (length, symbol ratio), classifier scores, perplexity-under-a-small-LM filters drop low-quality pages.
  4. Mixture design — blend web, books, code, Wikipedia, etc. with tuned sampling weights; the mixture shifts the "personality" of the base model.

Bad dedup inflates benchmark scores without real capability; bad filtering leaves toxicity and PII that later alignment must sand down.

Batching and curriculum (ops detail). Pretraining packs variable-length sequences into fixed contexts with padding or packing (concatenate short docs with attention masks). Packing improves GPU utilization. Some runs use curriculum (easy→hard) or stage mixtures (ramp code fraction)—effects are modest versus data volume and dedup. Optimizer is usually AdamW with warmup + cosine decay; stability matters more at 100B+ scale than the exact schedule name.

Tokenizer coupling: the same bytes→tokens map defines both the loss target and the perplexity unit. Comparing PPL across tokenizers (BPE vs SentencePiece, different vocab sizes) is an apples-oranges error in interviews.

Common mistake

Treating pretraining corpora as "more is always better" without dedup. Kaplan-era runs sometimes under-trained huge models; Chinchilla reframed the tradeoff (next section).

#Scaling laws: Kaplan vs Chinchilla

Kaplan et al. (OpenAI, 2020): loss scales as a power law in model size NNN, dataset size DDD (tokens), and compute CCC. With data held relatively fixed, bigger models win—if you can pay the FLOPs.

Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al., DeepMind, 2022): for a fixed compute budget CCC, the compute-optimal frontier balances NNN and DDD. Rule of thumb: train on about

D≈20 ND \approx 20\,ND≈20N

tokens (order-of-magnitude; exact constant depends on architecture and loss). Many "70B" models were historically under-trained relative to their parameter count; Chinchilla says shrink the model and feed it more tokens instead.

Compute estimate. One training step over DDD tokens on an NNN-parameter model costs roughly

C≈6 N D FLOPsC \approx 6\,N\,D \ \text{FLOPs}C≈6ND FLOPs

The factor 6 counts forward + backward through the dense transformer (matmul-heavy); constants vary with activation checkpointing, MoE sparsity, and hardware utilization. Interview use: given NNN and a FLOPs budget, back-solve DDD; given Chinchilla, check whether your token count is in the right ballpark.

Lens Emphasis Practical takeaway
Kaplan Scale NNN with compute Big models need proportionally more data than early runs used
Chinchilla ~20 tokens/param at iso-compute For a fixed training budget, a smaller model on more tokens beats a big under-trained one; production models deliberately go far past 20 tokens/param because inference cost scales with NNN, not DDD

#Emergent abilities: caveat

Plots of benchmark jumps at certain scales are seductive but controversial. Many "emergent" curves are metric artifacts (discrete accuracy on small test sets) or reflect evaluation noise. Smooth intrinsic losses improve gradually; capability thresholds depend on how you measure. In interviews: acknowledge scale helps, but don't claim mystic phase transitions as settled science.

Rapid fire — Pretraining

Objective: causal LM = average token cross-entropy. PPL =exp⁡(CE)= \exp(\text{CE})=exp(CE). Data: dedup + filter + mixture. Kaplan: power laws in NNN, DDD, CCC. Chinchilla: ~20 tokens/param at iso-compute. Training FLOPs ≈6ND\approx 6ND≈6ND. Emergent abilities: treat skeptically—metrics and smooth loss often disagree. Pretrain is self-supervised next-token MLE (Fundamentals §self-supervised).

#After pretraining: SFT and instruction tuning

Base models complete text; users want models that follow instructions. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) / instruction tuning finetunes on (prompt,ideal response)(\text{prompt}, \text{ideal response})(prompt,ideal response) pairs—often thousands to millions of curated examples plus synthetic data from stronger teachers.

Loss is the same token CE, but only on response tokens (prompt tokens masked in the loss). This teaches format, tool-use syntax, refusals, and tone. SFT is still supervised ERM (Empirical Risk Minimization) with a narrower distribution.

SFT alone does not encode human preferences between two valid answers—that is the alignment stage. It also does not inject fresh factual knowledge reliably; for facts, see RAG or targeted fine-tune.

Interview angle

"What's the difference between pretrain and SFT?" — distribution shift: pretrain mimics the internet; SFT mimics user-assistant conversations. Same loss, different data mask and mixture.*

Memory hook

SFT = same CE, mask the prompt, train the assistant turn.

#Alignment: RLHF, DPO, and alternatives

Alignment optimizes preferences: which completion humans (or rubrics) like better, not just which completion exists in the dataset.

#Preference data and the reward model (RLHF stage 1)

Collect pairs (x,yw,yl)(x, y_w, y_l)(x,yw​,yl​): prompt xxx, chosen ywy_wyw​, rejected yly_lyl​. Train a scalar reward model rϕ(x,y)r_\phi(x, y)rϕ​(x,y) with the Bradley–Terry pairwise loss:

LRM=−E(x,yw,yl)[log⁡σ(rϕ(x,yw)−rϕ(x,yl))]\mathcal{L}_{\text{RM}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\left[\log \sigma\big(r_\phi(x, y_w) - r_\phi(x, y_l)\big)\right]LRM​=−E(x,yw​,yl​)​[logσ(rϕ​(x,yw​)−rϕ​(x,yl​))]

This is logistic regression on the margin between scores—identical in spirit to pairwise ranking losses in Loss Functions. The RM is usually a pretrained LM with a value head; it must generalize to completions unlike its training pairs or the next stage collapses.

#RLHF stage 2: PPO with a KL anchor

Policy πθ\pi_\thetaπθ​ is the language model. PPO (or similar policy-gradient methods) increases expected reward E[rϕ(x,y)]\mathbb{E}[r_\phi(x,y)]E[rϕ​(x,y)] while penalizing drift from a frozen reference πref\pi_{\text{ref}}πref​ (typically the SFT checkpoint):

max⁡θ Ey∼πθ[rϕ(x,y)]−β Ey∼πθ[log⁡πθ(y∣x)πref(y∣x)]\max_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}\big[r_\phi(x,y)\big] - \beta\,\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}\left[\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)}\right]θmax​ Ey∼πθ​​[rϕ​(x,y)]−βEy∼πθ​​[logπref​(y∣x)πθ​(y∣x)​]

The KL term is the practical fix for reward hacking: without it, policies exploit RM blind spots—overlong answers, sycophancy, formatting tricks, or degenerate text that still scores high. β\betaβ trades improvement vs staying on-distribution.

Challenges: RM bias, PPO instability, expensive online rollouts, careful batching of generations. RLHF vs DPO card contrasts this with direct preference optimization.

PPO in one paragraph (interview depth). Policy-gradient RL maximizes reward but has high variance. PPO clips the ratio rt(θ)=πθ(at∣st)/πold(at∣st)r_t(\theta)=\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)/\pi_{\text{old}}(a_t\mid s_t)rt​(θ)=πθ​(at​∣st​)/πold​(at​∣st​) so large policy updates that would wreck the KL budget get attenuated. In LLM RL, "actions" are tokens (or whole responses treated as macro-actions), states are prompts plus partial generations, and the value baseline reduces variance. Production stacks batch thousands of completions per step—generation dominates wall-clock. That is why simpler offline losses (DPO) gained traction when reward is purely preference-shaped.

Reward hacking examples worth naming: endless bullet lists, agreeing with false premises, repeating the prompt, or exploiting RM's love of length and markdown. Mitigations: length penalties, diverse RM ensembles, held-out human spot checks, and KL—not optional decoration.

#DPO: preferences without an explicit RM

Direct Preference Optimization reparameterizes the optimal reward so the policy update becomes a binary classification on preference pairs. With hyperparameter β\betaβ (same role as KL strength),

LDPO=−E(x,yw,yl)[log⁡σ(βlog⁡πθ(yw∣x)πref(yw∣x)−βlog⁡πθ(yl∣x)πref(yl∣x))]\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y_w,y_l)}\left[\log \sigma\left(\beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)} - \beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right]LDPO​=−E(x,yw​,yl​)​[logσ(βlogπref​(yw​∣x)πθ​(yw​∣x)​−βlogπref​(yl​∣x)πθ​(yl​∣x)​)]

Why it matters: no separate RM training loop, no PPO rollout memory—just weighted log-likelihood ratios on chosen vs rejected. The implicit reward is βlog⁡πθπref\beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}βlogπref​πθ​​. Failure modes: sensitivity to β\betaβ, mismatch between SFT and preference data, and offline bias (no on-policy exploration).

#RLAIF, constitutional AI, verifiable rewards

RLAIF replaces human labels with AI judge labels—scale with model bias risk. Constitutional AI uses written principles to critique/revise outputs before preference learning. RL with verifiable rewards shines when correctness is checkable: unit tests for code (HumanEval-style), math answer keys, tool outcomes. GRPO (group relative policy optimization) compares multiple sampled completions per prompt and updates relative to the group mean—useful when scalar RM is weak but pass/fail verifiers exist.

#Alignment data hygiene

Preference datasets are smaller than pretrain corpora but leakier: duplicate prompts, annotator skew, and positional bias (humans prefer first shown answer). Decontaminate against benchmarks, balance task types, and track inter-annotator agreement. For DPO, keep the reference πref\pi_{\text{ref}}πref​ fixed to the SFT snapshot you intend to stay near—retraining SFT without re-collecting preferences desyncs the implicit KL target.

Where SFT ends and alignment begins: SFT teaches existence of a good answer format; alignment teaches relative quality among plausible answers. Skipping SFT and jumping to DPO on a cold base model usually fails because πref\pi_{\text{ref}}πref​ is not yet a competent assistant.

Go deeper (optional)

Process supervision (reward each reasoning step) and rejection sampling fine-tuning sit between SFT and full RL—worth mentioning if the interviewer cares about reasoning models.

Rapid fire — Alignment

Bradley–Terry RM: −log⁡σ(rw−rl)-\log\sigma(r_w - r_l)−logσ(rw​−rl​). PPO: maximize reward minus β\betaβ KL to πref\pi_{\text{ref}}πref​. KL fights reward hacking. DPO: sigmoid on βΔlog⁡(π/πref)\beta\Delta\log(\pi/\pi_{\text{ref}})βΔlog(π/πref​); no explicit RM. IPO/KTO/ORPO: unpaired or one-shot variants. RLAIF/constitutional: AI labels + principles. Verifiable rewards + GRPO when you can check answers. Evaluate with LLM Eval and human side-by-side prefs.

#Parameter-efficient fine-tuning

Full fine-tuning of a 70B model is often prohibitive. PEFT trains a small adapter while freezing most weights.

#LoRA

For a frozen weight W∈Rdout×dinW \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}W∈Rdout​×din​, LoRA adds a low-rank update:

W′=W+αr BA,B∈Rdout×r, A∈Rr×dinW' = W + \frac{\alpha}{r}\, B A,\quad B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r},\ A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}W′=W+rα​BA,B∈Rdout​×r, A∈Rr×din​

Train only AAA and BBB; r≪min⁡(din,dout)r \ll \min(d_{\text{in}}, d_{\text{out}})r≪min(din​,dout​). Scaling α/r\alpha/rα/r keeps effective step size stable as rank changes. Common targets: WQ,WK,WV,WOW_Q, W_K, W_V, W_OWQ​,WK​,WV​,WO​ in each attention block; sometimes FFN projections for hard domain adaptation. See LoRA & Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning.

Parameter count (worked). Per adapted matrix:

NLoRA=r (din+dout)N_{\text{LoRA}} = r\,(d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}})NLoRA​=r(din​+dout​)

vs full Nfull=dindoutN_{\text{full}} = d_{\text{in}} d_{\text{out}}Nfull​=din​dout​. Example: din=dout=4096d_{\text{in}} = d_{\text{out}} = 4096din​=dout​=4096, r=16r = 16r=16:

NLoRA=16×8192=131,072vsNfull=16,777,216N_{\text{LoRA}} = 16 \times 8192 = 131{,}072 \quad\text{vs}\quad N_{\text{full}} = 16{,}777{,}216NLoRA​=16×8192=131,072vsNfull​=16,777,216

Four attention matrices per layer: ≈5.2×105\approx 5.2 \times 10^5≈5.2×105 trainable parameters. A 32-layer stack: ≈1.7×107\approx 1.7 \times 10^7≈1.7×107 LoRA params vs ≈2.15×109\approx 2.15 \times 10^9≈2.15×109 if you trained all attention weights—~0.8% of attention alone, and ≪0.3% of a 7B model. Savings multiply because optimizers touch only trainable tensors.

#QLoRA, adapters, prompts

QLoRA stores base weights in 4-bit (NF4) with LoRA adapters in fp16/bf16—fits large-model tuning on one consumer GPU. Adapters insert small bottleneck FFNs per layer. Prefix / prompt tuning learns soft prompt embeddings; zero weight delta, least expressive but cheapest.

Interview angle

Strong LoRA answer includes where (Q/K/V/O), rank, α\alphaα, and a numeric savings estimate—not just "low-rank matrices."

#Choosing: full fine-tune vs LoRA vs prompting vs RAG

Need Lean toward Why
New stable facts at scale [[retrieval-augmented-generation RAG]] or refresh pretrain
Behavior / tone / format SFT, LoRA, or DPO Preference and instruction data
Domain style (legal, medical) LoRA or full FT on domain corpus Representation shift; watch forgetting
One-off task description Prompting / few-shot Zero train cost; context-limited
Max quality on narrow task, plenty GPU Full fine-tune Highest capacity; catastrophic forgetting risk

Catastrophic forgetting: aggressive FT erases general capabilities. Mitigate with small LR, early stopping, elastic weight consolidation, mixing general replay data, or LoRA that leaves base weights intact.

Distillation: train a smaller student to match a larger teacher (soft labels or hidden states). Used for cost at inference after you have an aligned teacher—pairs with LLM Inference economics.

Two common patterns:

  1. Response distillation — teacher generates targets; student minimizes CE to those tokens (plus optional KL to teacher logits for richness).
  2. Preference distillation — teacher ranks pairs; student trained with DPO-style loss using teacher as judge—cheap scale before human polish.

Distillation is not alignment by itself: if the teacher hallucinates, the student learns fluent falsehood faster.

#Continued pretraining and "mid-training"

Between base pretrain and SFT, some recipes run continued pretraining on higher-quality mixtures (more books, code, math) without instruction formatting—sometimes called mid-training. Goal: lift reasoning and domain knowledge before spending instruction tokens. This is still causal LM, not RL. It competes with RAG for facts: mid-training bakes knowledge in but freezes at cutoff; RAG stays fresh.

Interview angle

"How would you add new product docs?" — index + RAG for daily updates; optional LoRA on support tone; full continued pretrain only if the domain is the product (e.g., medical base model).

Rapid fire — PEFT & choices

LoRA: W′=W+(α/r)BAW' = W + (\alpha/r)BAW′=W+(α/r)BA; params r(din+dout)r(d_{\text{in}}+d_{\text{out}})r(din​+dout​) per matrix. QLoRA: 4-bit base + fp16 adapters. Adapters/prefix: even fewer params. RAG for facts; FT/DPO for behavior. Forgetting: mix replay or use LoRA. Distill for cheap deployment.

#Distributed training and memory math

Billion-parameter training never fits one GPU. Three orthogonal splits:

  1. Data parallelism — same weights, different micro-batches; all-reduce gradients (DDP).
  2. Tensor parallelism — split individual layers across devices (Megatron-style).
  3. Pipeline parallelism — split layers across stages; micro-batches fill the pipeline bubble.

ZeRO / FSDP shard optimizer states, gradients, and weights across ranks so no single GPU holds full Adam state—critical for large models without tensor parallel.

#Bytes per parameter (training)

Mixed-precision AdamW commonly allocates, per trainable parameter:

Component Typical storage
Weight (fp16/bf16) 2 B
Gradient (fp16) 2 B
Adam mmm, vvv (fp32) 8 B
Master weight (fp32, optional) 4 B

Interview rule of thumb: ≈12\approx 12≈12 bytes/param for fp16/bf16 weights + fp16 grads + fp32 Adam (m,vm,vm,v); ≈16\approx 16≈16 bytes/param when you also keep an fp32 master copy of weights (the usual mixed-precision recipe—matches 7×109×16≈1127 \times 10^9 \times 16 \approx 1127×109×16≈112 GB below). Activation memory adds separately (scales with batch, sequence length, layers; gradient checkpointing trades compute for activations).

Example: 7B full FT ⇒7×109×16≈112\Rightarrow 7 \times 10^9 \times 16 \approx 112⇒7×109×16≈112 GB optimizer + weights + grads alone—why ZeRO-3 or LoRA is mandatory on 8×80GB.

Worked Chinchilla sanity check. Suppose N=7×109N = 7\times 10^9N=7×109 parameters and Chinchilla suggests D≈20N≈1.4×1011D \approx 20N \approx 1.4\times 10^{11}D≈20N≈1.4×1011 tokens (~140B). Training FLOPs ≈6ND≈6×7×109×1.4×1011≈5.9×1021\approx 6ND \approx 6 \times 7\times 10^9 \times 1.4\times 10^{11} \approx 5.9\times 10^{21}≈6ND≈6×7×109×1.4×1011≈5.9×1021 FLOPs. A 7B model trained on only 30B tokens is badly under-trained for its size. Going the other way is common and deliberate: Llama 2 7B saw ~2T tokens (~290 tokens/param), "over-training" relative to Chinchilla because a smaller model that is cheaper to serve is worth extra training compute. Chinchilla answers "best loss per training FLOP", not "best model to deploy".

Sequence parallelism & checkpointing also matter for long contexts: activations scale with batch ×\times× layers ×\times× seq ×\times× hidden; gradient checkpointing recomputes activations in backward pass to shave memory at ~20–30% extra compute. For inference serving memory see KV Cache—training and serving budgets differ.

Rapid fire — Distributed & memory

Data parallel: shard batches, sync grads. Tensor parallel: shard layers. Pipeline: shard depth. ZeRO/FSDP: shard optimizer states. FLOPs ≈6ND\approx 6ND≈6ND.
