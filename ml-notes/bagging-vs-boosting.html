Bagging vs Boosting

Say it in 30s: Both are ensembles of many models, but they attack different errors. Bagging trains many strong, high-variance models (e.g. deep trees) in parallel on bootstrap samples and averages them — it reduces variance. Boosting trains weak, high-bias models (e.g. shallow trees) sequentially, each correcting the previous one's mistakes — it mainly reduces bias (and some variance).

Bagging (e.g. Random Forest)

  • Independent trees on resampled data; predictions averaged/voted.
  • Random Forest adds feature subsampling to decorrelate trees.
  • Robust, parallelizable, hard to overfit by adding trees.

Boosting (e.g. AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost/LightGBM)

  • Each learner focuses on the residuals/errors of the ensemble so far.
  • Often the strongest tabular performers, but more tuning and can overfit if over-trained.
  • Sequential → less parallelizable.

One-liner: bagging = many independent opinions averaged (variance↓); boosting = a relay team fixing each other's mistakes (bias↓).
