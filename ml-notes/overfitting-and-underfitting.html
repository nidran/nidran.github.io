Overfitting & Underfitting

Say it in 30s: Underfitting = the model is too simple to capture the signal (high training error AND high test error). Overfitting = the model memorizes the training sample including its noise (low training error, high test error). You diagnose which one you have from the gap between training and validation curves.

Reading the learning curve

  • Big gap that doesn't close with more data → high variance / overfitting → more data, regularization, simpler model.
  • Both curves plateau high and sit close together → high bias / underfitting → richer model or better features (more data won't help).

Fixes

  • Overfitting: regularization, dropout, early stopping, data augmentation, more data, ensembling.
  • Underfitting: more capacity, better/more features, train longer, reduce regularization.

The math

Train vs validation error: underfitting → both high and close; overfitting → training L\mathcal{L}L low, validation L\mathcal{L}L high (the gap widens with capacity).

Polynomial degree ddd: rich H\mathcal{H}H fits noise → R^n↓\hat{R}_n\downarrowR^n​↓, R↑R\uparrowR↑ past some ddd. Tame it with regularized ERM:

min⁡w  1n∑iL(w⊤ϕd(xi),yi)+λ∥w∥22.\min_{w}\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathcal{L}\big(w^\top\phi_d(x_i), y_i\big) + \lambda\lVert w\rVert_2^2.wmin​n1​i∑​L(w⊤ϕd​(xi​),yi​)+λ∥w∥22​.

Complexity penalties when comparing models fit by MLE / least squares (residual SSE, kkk params, nnn points):

AIC=nln⁡SSEn+2k,BIC=nln⁡SSEn+kln⁡n.\mathrm{AIC} = n\ln\frac{\mathrm{SSE}}{n} + 2k,\qquad \mathrm{BIC} = n\ln\frac{\mathrm{SSE}}{n} + k\ln n.AIC=nlnnSSE​+2k,BIC=nlnnSSE​+klnn.

BIC penalizes extra parameters harder for large nnn — favors simpler models when the gap is mostly noise.

Trap: a low training error alone tells you nothing. Always quote the validation gap.
