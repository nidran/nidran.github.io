Time Series · quick answer · 1 min read

Stationarity

Say it in 30s: A weakly stationary series has constant mean, constant variance, and autocovariance that depends only on lag kkk, not calendar time. Most raw business series trend and season — so ARIMA starts with differencing (ddd) until ADF (or KPSS) says you're stable enough to fit AR/MA terms. Over-differencing adds fake structure; under-differencing leaves unit roots and nonsense forecasts.

Key points

  • Trend → remove via differencing or detrending; seasonality → seasonal differencing yt−yt−my_t - y_{t-m}yt​−yt−m​.
  • ADF: null = unit root (non-stationary); small ppp → reject unit root. KPSS: null = stationarity; small ppp → reject stationarity (still needs differencing or detrending).
  • Stationarity is the license for fixed ARMA coefficients; breaks under regime change → drift monitoring in production forecasts.

The math

Weak stationarity: E[Yt]=μ\mathbb{E}[Y_t]=\muE[Yt​]=μ constant, Var(Yt)=σ2\mathrm{Var}(Y_t)=\sigma^2Var(Yt​)=σ2 finite and constant, and Cov(Yt,Yt−k)\mathrm{Cov}(Y_t, Y_{t-k})Cov(Yt​,Yt−k​) depends only on lag kkk, not ttt.

Differencing removes polynomial trend: ∇Yt=Yt−Yt−1\nabla Y_t = Y_t - Y_{t-1}∇Yt​=Yt​−Yt−1​; seasonal ∇mYt=Yt−Yt−m\nabla_m Y_t = Y_t - Y_{t-m}∇m​Yt​=Yt​−Yt−m​.

Random walk Yt=Yt−1+εtY_t = Y_{t-1} + \varepsilon_tYt​=Yt−1​+εt​: Var(Yt)=t σε2\mathrm{Var}(Y_t) = t\,\sigma_\varepsilon^2Var(Yt​)=tσε2​ grows with ttt → not stationary; first difference ∇Yt=εt\nabla Y_t = \varepsilon_t∇Yt​=εt​ often is.

ADF test regression:

ΔYt=α+βt+γYt−1+∑i=1pδiΔYt−i+εt\Delta Y_t = \alpha + \beta t + \gamma Y_{t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{p} \delta_i \Delta Y_{t-i} + \varepsilon_tΔYt​=α+βt+γYt−1​+i=1∑p​δi​ΔYt−i​+εt​

H0 ⁣:γ=0H_0\colon \gamma = 0H0​:γ=0 (unit root, i.e. non-stationary). A small ppp-value rejects H0H_0H0​: the series you tested is stationary enough for ARMA. Failing to reject means difference it and test again — the opposite of KPSS, whose null is stationarity.

Common follow-ups / traps

  • "Can I use random train/test split?" → No — time order; see Time-Series Validation & Backtesting.
  • "Is seasonality non-stationarity?" → Seasonal strength can be stationary after seasonal differencing or Fourier terms (Prophet).
