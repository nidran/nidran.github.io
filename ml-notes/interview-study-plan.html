Interview Study Plan

How to use this handbook: what to read for which interview round, in what order, and how to know you're ready.

ML interviews are not one exam. Most loops have four or five distinct rounds, each graded on something different: breadth of fundamentals, depth on models, coding, open-ended system design, and (increasingly) LLMs. The notes here are organized by topic, but you should study by round. This page maps one to the other.

What you'll get from this page
  • A reading order for each interview round, from must-know to nice-to-have.
  • Three priority tiers so you know what to cut when time is short.
  • A self-test for each round: if you can answer these out loud, you're ready.

#How the notes are built

Every topic comes in two sizes:

  • Chapters go deep: intuition, math, worked examples, interview angles, and a one-page summary at the end. Read these once, carefully.
  • Quick answers are one-minute cards with a "say it in 30 seconds" answer, key points, and the follow-ups interviewers ask. Re-read these the night before.

Tick Mark as reviewed at the bottom of a page to track progress; the sidebar greys out what you've finished. Press / to search.

Interview angle

Interviewers rarely ask "define X". They ask "why X and not Y?" and "what breaks X?". Every chapter ends with traps and trade-offs for exactly this reason. Practise answering out loud, not just reading.

#Round-by-round reading order

#ML fundamentals / breadth round

The "rapid-fire" round: 20–30 short questions across the whole field.

  1. Fundamentals — ERM, generalization, bias-variance, problem framing.
  2. Bias-Variance Tradeoff, Overfitting & Underfitting, Regularization, Generalization — the four cards you'll use in every other round.
  3. Data Preparation & Feature Engineering with Data Leakage and Class Imbalance.
  4. Model Evaluation & Validation with Precision & Recall, ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC, Cross-Validation, Calibration.
  5. Loss Functions and Cross-Entropy Loss.

#Theory / math round

  1. Math — linear algebra, probability, statistics, optimization, information theory.
  2. Maximum Likelihood Estimation and Gradient Descent.
  3. Eigenvectors, PCA and SVD.
  4. Probability & Statistics and p-Values & Statistical Power — statistics questions show up in almost every loop.
  5. Probability & Math Drills — practise out loud.

#Models round

  1. Regression Methods and Classification Methods.
  2. Decision Trees, Random Forests & Gradient Boosting — the most-asked model family for tabular data.
  3. Clustering, Dimensionality Reduction & Anomaly Detection.
  4. Cards to drill: Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines, Naive Bayes, k-Nearest Neighbors, Decision Trees, Bagging vs Boosting, Gradient Boosting, k-Means Clustering, Generative vs Discriminative.

#Coding round

Implement-from-scratch problems, usually in numpy or PyTorch.

  1. Coding Round: ML From Scratch — 14 problems (regression, k-means, attention, AUC, training loop) with tested solutions.
  2. PyTorch and TensorGym Study Notes — tensor shapes, eval() vs no_grad(), mixed precision.
  3. Tutorials & Cheatsheets — runnable scikit-learn and PyTorch guides.

#Deep learning round

  1. Training Neural Networks — activations, backprop, optimizers, normalization, debugging.
  2. PyTorch and TensorGym Study Notes — for the implementation round.
  3. Architectures: Vision beyond CNNs, CNNs, RNNs → LSTM / GRU, GNNs.
  4. Cards: Backpropagation, Optimizers: SGD, Momentum & Adam, BatchNorm vs LayerNorm, Dropout, Vanishing & Exploding Gradients, Activation Functions.

#LLM / GenAI round

  1. Transformers & Attention — non-negotiable for any modern ML role.
  2. LLM Training: Pretraining, Fine-Tuning & Alignment.
  3. LLM Inference: Decoding, KV Cache & Serving.
  4. Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
  5. LLM Evaluation.
  6. Cards: Self-Attention, Tokenization: BPE, WordPiece & SentencePiece, Decoding Strategies, KV Cache, LoRA & Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning, RLHF vs DPO, Embeddings & Vector Search, Hallucination.

#ML system design round

  1. ML System Design Framework — the six-step spine to drive every answer.
  2. ML System Design Interview Prep — each stage in depth.
  3. Recommender Systems — the most common prompt family (feeds, ads, search).
  4. ML in Production — serving, monitoring, drift, retraining.
  5. A/B Testing & Online Experimentation — how you prove the system works.
  6. Models and Loss Functions Reference — the lookup table. Worked cases: Search Ranking, Fraud and Abuse Detection, RAG Assistant; also Responsible ML, Bandits & RL, Causal Inference.
  7. Cards: Two-Tower Retrieval, Cold Start, Training-Serving Skew, Data Drift vs Concept Drift, Ranking Metrics: NDCG, MAP & MRR.

#Forecasting (role-dependent)

Time Series Stationarity, then Stationarity, ARIMA vs Exponential Smoothing, Time-Series Validation & Backtesting.

#Priority tiers

When time is short, cut from the bottom.

Tier Read Why
1 — Must know Fundamentals, Model Evaluation, Loss Functions, Trees & Ensembles, Training Neural Networks, Transformers & Attention, ML System Design Framework, and every quick answer Asked in nearly every loop; weak answers here are disqualifying
2 — Expected for mid/senior Math, Regression/Classification Methods, A/B Testing, Recommender Systems, ML in Production, LLM Training, RAG, Data Preparation Separates "knows the words" from "has shipped models"
3 — Role-dependent CNN/RNN/LSTM/GRU/GNN, LLM Inference, LLM Eval, Unsupervised Learning, Time Series, PCA/SVD Go deep only if the job description points there
Memory hook

Breadth first, then depth where the job is. One shallow pass over Tier 1 beats a perfect read of one chapter.

#Self-test: are you ready?

Answer each out loud in under two minutes, without notes.

Fundamentals

  • Decompose expected squared error into bias, variance, and noise. What moves each term?
  • Your validation AUC is great and production performance is poor. List five causes.
  • Why is accuracy the wrong metric at 1% prevalence, and what would you use?

Models

  • Walk through how gradient boosting fits the next tree. What does XGBoost add?
  • Why do random forests rarely overfit as you add trees, but boosting can?
  • When would you pick logistic regression over gradient boosting in production?

Deep learning

  • Why does Adam need bias correction? What does AdamW change?
  • What does BatchNorm do differently at train and eval time, and why do transformers use LayerNorm?
  • Your loss is NaN after 200 steps. What do you check, in order?

LLMs

  • Write scaled dot-product attention and explain the dk\sqrt{d_k}dk​​.
  • What is stored in the KV cache, how big is it, and how does GQA shrink it?
  • RLHF vs DPO: what does DPO remove, and what does it give up?
  • Your RAG system answers confidently but wrong. How do you find out whether retrieval or generation is at fault?

System design

  • Design a feed recommender: objective, metrics, candidate generation, ranking, serving, and how you'd A/B test it.
  • How do you detect and respond to data drift when labels arrive two weeks late?
  • How many users per arm do you need to detect a 1-point lift on a 10% conversion rate?
Rapid fire — How to study
  • Study by round, not by chapter order.
  • Chapters once, deeply; quick answers repeatedly.
  • Say answers out loud; interviews grade explanation, not recognition.
  • For every concept, know one trade-off and one failure mode.
  • In system design, drive the structure yourself.
