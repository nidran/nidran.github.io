Dropout

Say it in 30s: Dropout randomly zeroes activations during training with probability ppp, which prevents co-adaptation of neurons—like training an ensemble of thinned nets that share weights. Use inverted dropout: keep each unit with prob 1−p1-p1−p and multiply survivors by 1/(1−p)1/(1-p)1/(1−p) so the expected activation matches eval. At test time, dropout is off and you use the full network without scaling.

Key points

  • Training mask m∼Bernoulli(1−p)m \sim \text{Bernoulli}(1-p)m∼Bernoulli(1−p); activation h′=h⊙m/(1−p)h' = h \odot m / (1-p)h′=h⊙m/(1−p) (inverted form).
  • Typical ppp: 0.2–0.5 on hidden layers; often lower or none on input/output.
  • model.train() applies dropout; model.eval() disables it—same discipline as BatchNorm.
  • Complements weight decay and data augmentation; less common in modern transformers (replaced partly by stochastic depth, LayerNorm, and huge data).

The math

Inverted dropout: mask m∼Bernoulli(1−p)m\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1-p)m∼Bernoulli(1−p), h~=m⊙h/(1−p)\tilde{h}=m\odot h/(1-p)h~=m⊙h/(1−p). Per unit, E[h~j]=hj\mathbb{E}[\tilde{h}_j]=h_jE[h~j​]=hj​ because E[mj]=1−p\mathbb{E}[m_j]=1-pE[mj​]=1−p. Conditional on hjh_jhj​, Var(h~j)=p1−phj2\mathrm{Var}(\tilde{h}_j)=\frac{p}{1-p}h_j^2Var(h~j​)=1−pp​hj2​ — extra noise during training. On a linear model, dropout on inputs is related to a data-dependent ridge-like penalty (not identical to fixed L2L_2L2​). MC dropout: keep random masks at test time and average predictions to approximate Bayesian model averaging.

Common follow-ups / traps

  • "Do you scale at test time by 1−p1-p1−p?" → No if you used inverted dropout during training (PyTorch default in nn.Dropout).
  • "Can dropout hurt a small dataset?" → Yes—can underfit; reduce ppp or drop dropout on the last layers.
