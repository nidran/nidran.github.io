Clustering, Dimensionality Reduction & Anomaly Detection

Unsupervised learning is structure-finding without labels — clusters, low-dimensional views, and rare events — and interviews test whether you know when each method's geometry matches the data.

With only inputs xxx and no yyy, algorithms hunt groupings, compressions, or outliers. This chapter covers clustering from k-means through density and hierarchy, dimensionality reduction beyond the PCA treatment in Eigenvectors, PCA & SVD, honest reading of t-SNE/UMAP plots, anomaly detection without labels, and evaluation with and without ground truth.

What you'll be able to say after this chapter
  • Run Lloyd's k-means, state its objective, and initialize with k-means++; name failure modes and how to pick kkk.
  • Walk EM for a GMM and explain k-means as a hard, equal-variance limit.
  • Contrast hierarchical, DBSCAN/HDBSCAN, and spectral clustering when blobs aren't spherical.
  • Place kernel PCA, t-SNE, UMAP, and autoencoders relative to PCA and state what 2D embeddings can and cannot prove.
  • Compare isolation forest, one-class SVM, LOF, reconstruction error, and simple z-score/IQR baselines.
  • Evaluate clusters with silhouette / Davies–Bouldin and, when labels exist, ARI/NMI; discuss anomaly metrics without labels.

#k-Means: Lloyd's Algorithm and Choosing k

#Objective and algorithm

k-means seeks kkk centroids {μj}\{\mu_j\}{μj​} and hard assignments minimizing

J=∑j=1k∑x∈Cj∥x−μj∥22J = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \sum_{x \in C_j} \Vert x - \mu_j \Vert_2^2J=j=1∑k​x∈Cj​∑​∥x−μj​∥22​

Lloyd's algorithm alternates:

  1. Assign: each point to nearest centroid.
  2. Update: μj=\mu_j =μj​= mean of points in cluster jjj.

Each step lowers JJJ or leaves it unchanged; convergence is to a local minimum — run multiple restarts.

k-means++ initialization picks centroids with probability proportional to squared distance to existing centers, spreading seeds and cutting worst-case iteration counts versus uniform random starts.

Memory hook

k-means = Voronoi diagram whose cell centers chase their means until they stop moving.

#Choosing kkk

Heuristic Idea Caveat
Elbow Plot JJJ vs kkk; bend suggests kkk Subjective; inertia always decreases
Silhouette Mean cohesion vs separation in [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1] Favors convex, separated blobs
Domain Business segments, capacity Often beats blind metrics

With external labels (rare in pure unsupervised, common in evaluation), use Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) or Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) — see §5.

#Failure modes

  • Wrong kkk: splits or merges natural groups.
  • Non-spherical / varying density: k-means assumes spherical, similar-variance clusters (see GMM/DBSCAN).
  • Outliers: pull centroids; winsorize, robust clustering, or density methods.
  • Scale: Euclidean distance dominated by large features — standardize continuous columns (Data Preparation & Feature Engineering).
Interview angle

"When does k-means fail?" → elongated/manifold clusters, unequal sizes/densities, wrong kkk, unscaled features. Offer DBSCAN, GMM, or spectral alternatives by geometry.

See also k-Means Clustering.

#Mini example: two clusters in 1D

Points {1,2,3,7,8,9}\{1,2,3, 7,8,9\}{1,2,3,7,8,9}, k=2k=2k=2. Initialization μ1=1\mu_1=1μ1​=1, μ2=9\mu_2=9μ2​=9:

  • Assign: {1,2,3}→C1\{1,2,3\}\to C_1{1,2,3}→C1​, {7,8,9}→C2\{7,8,9\}\to C_2{7,8,9}→C2​.
  • Update: μ1=2\mu_1=2μ1​=2, μ2=8\mu_2=8μ2​=8 — converged (assignments unchanged).

On data this clean almost any start converges to the same answer. Bad starts bite with more clusters: drop two initial centroids inside one true blob and none in another, and Lloyd converges with one blob split in two and two blobs merged into one cluster. Each step only moves centroids locally, so it never escapes that local optimum. That's why k-means++ and multiple restarts are defaults, not optional polish.

Rapid fire — k-means
  • Minimize within-cluster sum of squares; Lloyd assign/update.
  • Local optima → multiple restarts; k-means++ for init.
  • Elbow + silhouette + domain for kkk; scale features.
  • Spherical-blob assumption; use GMM/DBSCAN when geometry differs.

#GMMs and EM: Soft Assignment

A Gaussian Mixture Model models data as a weighted sum of Gaussians:

p(x)=∑j=1kπj N(x∣μj,Σj)p(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \pi_j\, \mathcal{N}(x \mid \mu_j, \Sigma_j)p(x)=j=1∑k​πj​N(x∣μj​,Σj​)

EM algorithm:

  • E-step: posterior responsibility γij=P(z=j∣xi)\gamma_{ij} = P(z=j \mid x_i)γij​=P(z=j∣xi​) from current parameters.
  • M-step: update πj\pi_jπj​, μj\mu_jμj​, Σj\Sigma_jΣj​ as weighted MLE with weights γij\gamma_{ij}γij​.

Output: soft cluster membership (probability per component), elliptical clusters when Σj\Sigma_jΣj​ are full.

k-means limit: equal Σj=σ2I\Sigma_j = \sigma^2 IΣj​=σ2I, responsibilities → hard 0/1, M-step → centroids as means — k-means is EM with a very stiff GMM.

k-means GMM
Assignment Hard Soft
Shape Spherical Elliptical (covariance)
Optimization Lloyd EM (local optimum)
Memory hook

k-means is a hard GMM wearing a spherical straightjacket.

BIC for choosing kkk: BIC=ln⁡n⋅∣θ∣−2ln⁡L^\mathrm{BIC} = \ln n \cdot |\theta| - 2 \ln \hat{L}BIC=lnn⋅∣θ∣−2lnL^ penalizes parameter count ∣θ∣|\theta|∣θ∣ (each extra component adds means, covariances, weights). Pick kkk with lowest BIC on a validation likelihood or full-data score — more principled than elbow when clusters overlap.

Interview angle

"k-means vs GMM?" → hard vs soft assignment; spherical vs elliptical covariances; Lloyd vs EM. Use GMM when blobs have different shapes/scales and you want uncertainty.

Rapid fire — GMM / EM
  • E-step: responsibilities; M-step: weighted means/covariances.
  • Soft memberships; BIC/AIC often used to pick component count kkk.
  • k-means ≈ equal spherical covariances + hard assignments.

#Hierarchical, DBSCAN, and Spectral Clustering

#Hierarchical clustering

Build a dendrogram by iteratively merging (agglomerative) or splitting (divisive) clusters.

Linkage chooses merge distance:

Linkage Merges when Behavior
Single Nearest pair across clusters Chaining, elongated clusters
Complete Farthest pair Compact clusters
Average Mean pairwise distance Balanced
Ward Smallest increase in within-cluster variance k-means-like, popular

Cut the dendrogram at a height to get kkk clusters — no need to specify kkk upfront, but choice of cut remains.

#DBSCAN and HDBSCAN

DBSCAN defines clusters as dense regions separated by sparse gaps:

  • ε\varepsilonε neighborhood radius, min_samples core threshold.
  • Core points have ≥\geq≥ min_samples neighbors within ε\varepsilonε; border points attach to cores; noise labeled −1-1−1.

Strengths: arbitrary shape, automatic noise, no fixed kkk. Weaknesses: sensitive to ε\varepsilonε in varying density — HDBSCAN builds a hierarchy over ε\varepsilonε and extracts stable clusters.

Interview angle

"DBSCAN vs k-means?" → density-defined arbitrary shapes + explicit noise vs centroid/spherical assumption.

#Spectral clustering (brief)

When clusters are non-convex but separable on a graph, build a similarity matrix Wij=exp⁡(−∥xi−xj∥2/2σ2)W_{ij} = \exp(-\Vert x_i - x_j \Vert^2 / 2\sigma^2)Wij​=exp(−∥xi​−xj​∥2/2σ2), take the bottom eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian, and k-means in that embedded space. This is the same eigenvector instinct as PCA (Eigenvectors, PCA & SVD) but on affinities, not raw covariance structure.

Rapid fire — Beyond k-means
  • Hierarchical: dendrogram + linkage; Ward ≈ variance-minimizing merges.
  • DBSCAN/HDBSCAN: density + noise; ε\varepsilonε tuning matters.
  • Spectral: graph eigenvectors for non-convex clusters.

#Dimensionality Reduction Beyond PCA

PCA (in Eigenvectors, PCA & SVD) finds orthogonal directions of max variance — linear, global, invertible (in the retained subspace). Several methods answer different questions.

#Kernel PCA

Maps xxx implicitly via kernel K(x,x′)K(x,x')K(x,x′) and runs PCA in feature space — nonlinear structure without explicit coordinates. Cost scales with n2n^2n2–n3n^3n3; choose kernel like an SVM (Support Vector Machines).

#t-SNE

Optimizes Student-t distribution in 2D/3D to match pairwise similarities in high dimensions (heavy tails avoid crowding). Great for visualizing local neighborhoods; not for:

  • Distances between distant clusters (global layout unreliable).
  • Downstream features — new points need out-of-sample extensions (e.g. parametric t-SNE) or refit.
  • Cluster size faithfully represented (exaggerates local density).

#UMAP

Builds a fuzzy topological representation of high-D data and optimizes a low-D layout — often faster than t-SNE, can preserve more global structure, still primarily a visualization tool. Hyperparameters (n_neighbors, min_dist) control local vs global tradeoff.

#Autoencoders

Neural encoder z=g(x)z = g(x)z=g(x) and decoder x^=h(z)\hat{x} = h(z)x^=h(z) trained to minimize reconstruction loss. The bottleneck zzz is a learned nonlinear embedding — useful for anomaly scores (reconstruction error) and as input to downstream models; needs more data and tuning than PCA.

Method Linear? Global structure Typical use
PCA Yes Yes Features, denoise, visualize PCs
Kernel PCA No (kernel) Moderate Nonlinear visualization
t-SNE No Local 2D exploration only
UMAP No Local + some global 2D exploration, sometimes features
Autoencoder No Learned Compression, anomalies, pretrain
What t-SNE/UMAP cannot tell you

Cluster count is not identifiable from a pretty 2D plot. Distance between blobs is not a rigorous inter-cluster metric. Always validate with quantitative metrics (silhouette on original space, stability across seeds, or labeled ARI/NMI if available).

Practical workflow: PCA or UMAP for EDA → pick clustering in original (or PCA-reduced) space → only then color clusters on a 2D embedding for storytelling. Never cluster on 2D t-SNE coordinates alone — the projection distortion becomes your loss function.

When pipelines need fixed-dimension features for downstream supervised models, prefer PCA, supervised reduced targets, or autoencoder embeddings with reconstruction monitored on validation. t-SNE/UMAP coordinates are not stable across refits unless you use parametric extensions.

Memory hook

PCA preserves variance; t-SNE/UMAP preserve neighborhoods for your eyes — not your production feature pipeline by default.

Rapid fire — Dimensionality reduction
  • PCA: linear, variance, see dedicated chapter.
  • Kernel PCA: nonlinear via kernels; costly in nnn.
  • t-SNE/UMAP: visualization-first; local structure; global distances suspect.
  • Autoencoders: learned bottleneck; need data; reconstruction for anomalies.

#Anomaly Detection Without Labels

Point anomalies are rare observations unlike the bulk of training data. Without labels, evaluation is hard — lean on domain review, stability, and proxy labels when you can.

#Classical baselines

  • z-score: flag ∣(x−μ)/σ∣>3\vert (x - \mu)/\sigma \vert > 3∣(x−μ)/σ∣>3 per feature — assumes univariate normality; breaks in multivariate tails.
  • IQR rule: outside [Q1−1.5 IQR, Q3+1.5 IQR][Q1 - 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR},\, Q3 + 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}][Q1−1.5IQR,Q3+1.5IQR] — robust univariate fences.

Good first screens; combine dimensions with multivariate methods below.

#Isolation Forest

Random trees isolate points by random splits; anomalies need fewer splits (short average path length). decision_function ~ inverse path length. Scales well; weak on very high-D without informative features.

#One-Class SVM

Finds a boundary around training data in feature (or kernel) space — points outside are anomalies. ν\nuν controls expected fraction of outliers. Sensitive to scaling and kernel choice; training cost grows with nnn.

#Local Outlier Factor (LOF)

Compares local density of a point to its neighbors' densities — flags points much sparser than neighbors (local anomalies). Can miss global rare points in uniformly sparse regions.

#Autoencoder reconstruction error

High ∥x−x^∥\Vert x - \hat{x} \Vert∥x−x^∥ on validation/production points suggests the model never learned that region — watch domain shift (Fundamentals on i.i.d.) masquerading as anomalies.

#Evaluating without labels

Approach What you get
Injected synthetic anomalies PR on held-out injected points
Historical incidents Partial precision if logged
Human audit top-kkk Operational precision proxy
Stability across seeds Consistent flags are more trustworthy

With sparse labels, report PR-AUC at low false-positive rates — see Precision & Recall and ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC.

Interview angle

"How do you validate anomaly detection with no labels?" → inject controlled outliers, audit top scores with SMEs, track alert stability, and if any incidents exist treat them as weak positives — never trust unsupervised scores as precision without measurement.

Rapid fire — Anomalies
  • z-score/IQR: fast univariate screens.
  • Isolation Forest: path length isolation; scalable.
  • One-class SVM: boundary in kernel space; tune ν\nuν, scale features.
  • LOF: local density contrast.
  • Autoencoder: reconstruction error; mind domain shift.
  • No labels → inject, audit, stability; PR at low FPR if any positives.

#Cluster and Embedding Evaluation

#Internal metrics (no labels)

  • Silhouette (per point): (b−a)/max⁡(a,b)(b - a)/\max(a,b)(b−a)/max(a,b) where aaa is mean intra-cluster distance, bbb is mean distance to nearest other cluster — average over points.
  • Davies–Bouldin: average worst ratio of within-cluster scatter to inter-centroid distance — lower is better; favors compact, separated clusters.

Both assume convex, separated geometry like k-means — misleading on manifolds.

#External metrics (labels available)

  • ARI: chance-adjusted agreement between partitions (1 = perfect, 0 = chance).
  • NMI: mutual information normalized — measures label information shared with clusters.

Use when you have partial labels (e.g. evaluating customer segments against known tiers).

Cluster stability: resample rows (bootstrap), re-cluster, and measure Adjusted Rand Index between runs or Jaccard overlap of pairs co-clustered. Stable structure survives noise; fragile partitions are interview red flags for "we found segments" claims without business validation.

Memory hook

Silhouette asks "am I closer to my cluster than to the neighbor's?"; ARI asks "do clusters match labels better than chance?"

Rapid fire — Evaluation
  • Silhouette ↑ better; Davies–Bouldin ↓ better — both favor blobby k-means geometry.
  • ARI/NMI when labels exist; chance adjustment matters for ARI.
  • t-SNE plots are not evaluation — run metrics in input space or with labels.
  • Bootstrap stability checks whether clusters are signal or seed noise.

#Unsupervised in Production and Interviews

Production unsupervised work is rarely "run k-means and ship." Typical patterns:

Use case Method sketch Pitfall
Customer segments k-means/GMM on RFM + scale Segments drift — re-fit on a schedule
Fraud / abuse Isolation Forest + rules Alert flood without PR at low FPR
Embeddings for search Autoencoder or pretrain Train/serve skew — see [[data-leakage
Catalog dedup HDBSCAN on embeddings Threshold tuning per category
Interview angle

"How many clusters?" → refuse a magic number — combine silhouette/BIC, stability across seeds, business interpretability, and downstream uplift (does a campaign per cluster beat one blast?). Metrics guide; owners decide.

Rapid fire — Production & interviews
  • Re-fit on drift; monitor cluster sizes and centroids over time.
  • Anomaly alerts need human review loops and explicit FPR budgets.
  • Cluster in input space; visualize in 2D; don't invert that order.
  • Partial labels + ARI beat eyeballing t-SNE.
